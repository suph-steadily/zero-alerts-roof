# Does the roof exclusion actually reduce what we pay? The loss join

*Run 2026-08-21. Closes the question `RESULTS-2026-08-20.md` reframed and left open: "is our
exposure reduced or only moved into the contract, which needs the loss join." Query:
[`sql/04_loss_join.sql`](sql/04_loss_join.sql), Metabase db 235, read-only.*

## The answer

**Yes, it does — but not by stopping the claim. It stops the payment.**

The exclusion has almost no effect on whether a wind or hail claim gets filed. It has a very
large effect on what we pay when one is. On the excluded book the median wind/hail claim pays
**$477** against **$7,523** for a comparable non-excluded claim in the same loss quarter, and
**83% of the roof-peril dollars a matched non-excluded book would have paid are not paid.**

This is the first measured *benefit* number for the dial, and it is a dollar figure rather
than a count of "harm prevented": **roughly $52 of avoided paid loss per excluded home-year**
(see the caveat on generalizing it in *What this does not license*, below).

## How it was measured

- **Cohort.** Bound new-business dwellings created 2024-01-01 to 2026-06-30, `year_built >
  1700`. 7,060 with the roof surfacing exclusion at bind, 250,691 without.
- **Exposure.** First term only: exposure capped at 365 days from bind, and a claim counts
  only if its `loss_date` falls inside that window. 3,905 excluded dwelling-years against
  186,378 non-excluded. This stops a young 2026 cohort being compared with a matured 2024 one.
- **Peril split.** `standardized_loss_type` in `('Hail','Wind')` is the roof-surfacing peril
  pair. Book-wide (deduped) Hail is the #1 loss type by claim count — 2,022 claims, $21.9M paid
  — and Wind #3 (1,355, $15.5M); together they are 46% of every claim we have. Every other
  peril is kept as a **control group**: the exclusion should not touch it, so it measures how
  much of any gap is just "these homes differ."
- **Expected loss.** Direct standardisation on **state x bind-half-year** cells: each cell's
  non-excluded rate applied to the excluded book's own exposure. State controls hail geography
  — this matters, the excluded book is under-weighted in Texas (4.3% of excluded dwellings vs
  12.6% of the rest) and over-weighted in CA/PA/NJ. Bind-half controls calendar time and so
  claim development.
- **Claims grain.** `dbt.claims_smga` is a monthly *cumulative snapshot*: 86,317 rows are only
  7,278 claims. Deduped to the latest file per `claim_id`; skipping this inflates every count
  about 12x. `dwelling_id` is the bare ipod dwelling uuid, so it joins straight to the
  roof-dial book.

## Actual vs expected on the excluded book (3,905 dwelling-years)

| | actual | expected | A/E | exact 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wind/hail claims | 25 | 31.3 | **0.80** | 0.52 - 1.18 |
| wind/hail **paid** | **$41,152** | **$316,333** | **0.130** | - |
| control-peril claims | 46 | 51.1 | 0.90 | 0.66 - 1.20 |
| control-peril paid | $641,073 | $833,011 | 0.770 | - |

Read the two rows against each other. On **counts**, the roof perils (0.80) are
indistinguishable from the control perils (0.90) and both intervals cross 1.0 — the exclusion
does not measurably stop claims arriving. On **dollars**, the roof perils fall to 0.130 while
the control perils only fall to 0.770. Netting the roof effect against the control group's own
0.770 leaves **0.169 — 83% of expected roof-peril dollars unpaid.**

## Where the money goes: severity, not frequency

Paid per wind/hail claim:

| | p25 | median | p75 | p90 | max | >= $50k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no exclusion (n=1,846) | $730 | **$7,523** | $14,896 | $23,042 | $621,406 | 35 (1.9%) |
| exclusion at bind (n=25) | $371 | **$477** | $807 | $5,235 | $14,848 | **0** |

The excluded distribution is not merely missing its tail — it is shifted down at every
quantile, 15.8x lower at the median. Mean severity is $11,395 against $1,646 (6.9x). Every one
of the 25 excluded wind/hail claims paid: `0, 0, 0, 31, 130, 366, 371, 399, 405, 444, 452,
468, 477, 481, 550, 563, 565, 661, 807, 1244, 1395, 4707, 5235, 6554, 14848`. Twenty-one of
25 came in under $1,400 — the size of an adjusting fee and a small interior item, not a roof.

**Development-matched significance.** Comparing each excluded claim to the non-excluded median
*of its own loss quarter* removes the objection that the excluded book's claims are simply
younger and less developed: **22 of 25 paid below their quarter's median** (null expectation
12.5), one-sided p = 7.8e-05. Two independent controls — standardisation and this
within-quarter test — agree.

**And the mechanism is visible.** `coverage_denied` is set on **16.0%** of excluded wind/hail
claims against **3.1%** of non-excluded (5.2x). The claim descriptions on the excluded book are
unmistakably roof claims still being filed: *"Hail damage to the roof."* — *"Roof blew off from
windstorm."* — *"Wind, hail, and ice damaged the roof, causing water to intrude all over the
house."* Those arrive, get adjusted, and pay a few hundred dollars.

So the exclusion is doing exactly what an exclusion is supposed to do, and it shows up in cash.
The memo's worry that a drop in roof NOCs might be "close to guaranteed by construction" was
right about NOCs — but the *paid dollars* are not definitional, and they move.

## What this does not license

1. **n = 25 excluded wind/hail claims, $41k of paid loss.** The dollar A/E has no interval
   here. The count A/E interval (0.52-1.18) is wide enough to include no frequency effect at
   all, which is consistent with the reading above but is not precision.
2. **$52/home-year is the benefit on hand-picked roofs, not on marginal ones.** These
   exclusions were applied by underwriters looking at imagery on the worst roofs they saw. A
   bar-80 automatic setting would exclude far more marginal roofs, whose avoided loss per home
   must be lower — probably much lower. Do not multiply $52 by a bar-80 catch count.
3. **The control perils fall to 0.770 and nothing here explains why.** Excluded homes pay less
   on water, fire and theft too. Until that is understood (Coverage A size? more underwriter
   attention overall? roof-origin water coded as Water Damage?), the netted 0.169 is the
   number to quote, not the raw 0.130.
4. **Exclusions added post-bind by endorsement sit in the control group,** because the flag is
   read off the issued NB quote row. That dilutes the control with excluded exposure and biases
   the measured effect *downward*, so it is conservative.
5. **Selection is not random** — but it runs the same way: underwriters picked the worst roofs,
   which should raise expected roof loss, not lower it. Also conservative for the direction
   found.
6. **First term only, and paid loss is undeveloped on recent claims** for both groups. The
   bind-half standardisation and the within-quarter test both address this; neither replaces a
   matured cohort.
7. **This prices only the benefit side.** The exchange rate the memo asks for still needs the
   cost side: bind loss, abandonment, override requests, post-bind removals, complaints, and
   the never-referred compliance lane. Nothing here says a bar is worth setting; it says the
   thing the bar controls has a real dollar effect.

## What it changes upstream

- Open item 4 in `RESULTS-2026-08-20.md` ("the exchange rate") now has a measured benefit unit
  on one side. It is still missing every cost term.
- The reframed pilot question stands and gets sharper: the exclusion's contractual bite is

  abandonment, overrides, removals, complaints), not on re-confirming that excluded roofs stop
  being paid for.
