# Roof dial: independent review of the first read

Review date: September 23, 2026. Source main: `4c28b36a762721ce59eb039029fffdb2fd103659`. Review branch: `codex-roof-review-2026-09-23`.

## Verdict

The first read establishes a useful workflow hypothesis: higher roof scores overlap with more historical exclusion decisions. It does not establish bar 83 as the best production choice, a causal bind benefit, or prevention of cancellation letters. Version pinning changes the candidate costs, a broader non-auto comparator removes the claimed bind advantage, and historical loss differences do not price the marginal 101+ roofs. Some important counts do reproduce, including zero model-exclude decisions among 106 all-age cancellation-transaction policies. Choose a bar only after reconstructing eligible decision-time quotes and application actors, measuring the attestation route, and agreeing how much an extra workflow catch and extra coverage restriction are worth. The sister-model question should be decided by ranking and equal-capacity capture tests, not medians alone.

## Decision-changing findings

| Finding | What it means |
|---|---|
| Model version and quote population change the curve | The historical pooled bound-home price is not the price of the proposed v1.2.0 quote rule. The live bound-home bootstrap does not establish a unique break at 85 or a best bar of 83. |
| Restoring the omitted hand group removes the claimed bind advantage | Treat the measured bind comparisons as selected associations. A fixed-window controlled pilot is still needed. |
| Texas has current exclusion signatures on quotes | The old bound-101+ zero is too narrow to diagnose a state rollout failure. Actor and form eligibility still need verification. |
| Roof-coded cancellation transactions are more frequent within the excluded 101+ policy group | The wording that the excess is not roof-related is misleading. Most absolute excess is unclassified, and inspection selection is unresolved. |
| Loss exposure has a real but small join bug | Corrected dates and later claims development change the estimates more. Lower payments in control perils also weaken a roof-specific causal interpretation. |
| The escape hatch changes the routing and evidence requirements | Current compliance counts cannot be added to a historical bound-home projection. Measure eligible traffic, residual alerts, attestation uptake and audited discrepancies. |

## What holds and what still gates the decision

The preview arithmetic holds for the committed aggregate fixture. A broader bar captures more homes whose issued rows carry hand-type exclusions and adds more exclusions to historical left-alone homes. The move from 85 to 83 buys 41 additional signature-labelled catches at 99 additional left-alone homes, or 2.415 per extra catch, in the May 1-Aug 15 bound 101+ population. It is not the cumulative 528/557=0.948 figure. Neither ratio prices customer acceptance, legal routing, roof condition truth or causal prevention.

Bar 83 is still unpriced for version-pinned eligible decision-time traffic. Current-row or issued-row reconstructions cannot supply a missing quote-version history. In particular, a `pol_created_timestamp` is a cohort date and `quote_issued_timestamp` is an issue event time; neither should be presented as a model-version arrival timestamp. The proposed SQL uses coherent issued tuples and reports ambiguity. This is a useful repair for the cross-column stitch, conditional on uniqueness. It does not recover historical score changes by itself.

A policy-level maximum score and any excluded home can be legitimate *separate policy features* if clearly labelled. They are not the features of one particular home. A corrected policy audit should report both a coherent high-score-home tuple and an explicit any-home exposure flag. A home-level harm claim additionally needs the affected-home key; copying a policy event to each home measures association only. The proposed overlay query names that limit.

The brief says assumeNotNull turns NULL into zero. The documented contract is weaker: a NULL input can produce an arbitrary value. Consequently the valid fix is explicit scored-only quantiles, with unscored counts alongside them. Merely assuming the inserted values are zeros is not reliable. [ClickHouse nullable-function documentation](https://clickhouse.com/docs/sql-reference/functions/functions-for-nulls#assumenotnull).

An implementable outline for the repaired overlay is below. `canonical_scores` is a validated policy-level record whose source home choice and version are explicit.
```python
def policy_overlay(events, canonical_scores, cohort, start, end, horizon, bars):
    eligible = {
        p for p, c in cohort.items()
        if start <= c.issue_date < end
        and c.observed_through >= c.issue_date + timedelta(days=horizon)
        and c.in_event_census
    }
    by_kind = {}
    for event in events:
        if event.policy_id not in eligible:
            continue
        c = cohort[event.policy_id]
        elapsed = (event.transaction_date - c.issue_date).days
        if 0 <= elapsed <= horizon:
            by_kind.setdefault(event.kind, set()).add(event.policy_id)
    for kind, policies in by_kind.items():
        scores = [canonical_scores[p].score for p in policies]
        for bar in bars:
            yield kind, bar, len(policies), sum(s is not None for s in scores), \
                sum(s is not None and s >= bar for s in scores)
```

R has the unit of catch value divided by net cost per extra left-alone exclusion, using one common horizon and population. For every draw the query compares each candidate with every other candidate, including the current setting, to find the full interval where its linear utility is maximal. It does not assume adjacent steps are ordered. Conditional on a candidate having a nonempty global interval, the 95% bootstrap ranges for its lower and upper endpoints are: bar 85 lower 0.96-1.60 and upper 1.17-2.16; bar 83 lower 1.10-2.03 and upper 1.48-2.61; bar 80 lower 1.43-2.46 and upper 1.96-3.15. These are separate endpoint distributions, not one combined confidence interval for a decision window; their conditioning and nonempty fractions must travel with them.

All 2,000 draws have positive extra catches at each non-status-quo step in this sample. The query nevertheless counts positive-cost/zero-catch steps separately as infinite cost, zero/zero steps as no change, and the status quo as having no preceding step. Conditional quantile arrays with zero eligible draws are ClickHouse defaults and must not be read as estimates. The lowest bar has no finite upper R bound, so its zero-filled finite-upper array is explicitly accompanied by a zero finite-draw count.

## Arithmetic verification recipe

All displayed historical rates were recomputed as numerator/denominator. Independent-quote difference intervals use `(p1-p0) +/-1.96*sqrt (p1*(1-p1)/n1+ p0*(1-p0)/n0)`. These are descriptive approximations and do not replace policy clustering. Rate-ratio intervals use `exp (log (RR) +/-1.96*sqrt (1/k1+1/k0))`. Fisher values were computed by summing hypergeometric probabilities no greater than the observed table probability, with log-gamma binomial coefficients, including B13p=0.10719, B14p=0.14162, O5p=0.08978, O6p=0.05145 and N9E3p=0.05404. The original count tables are sufficient inputs; no raw records are needed. The issue-clock decade ratio was recomputed from the 1392 aggregate cells, retaining unmatched exposure separately. The bootstrap implementation in memos_noc_bootstrap.sql uses one policy as the sampling unit, resamples with replacement within decade/state/issue-half-year/lane cells, and outputs only aggregate uncertainty. It keeps actual events and exposure together and recomputes expected events on every draw.

## Findings that change the decision

**Do not choose a production bar from the matrix's thin R windows.** Its marginal arithmetic largely holds conditional on W_D counts, but the implementation substitutes rounded ratios, starts after an unpriced first step and greedily walks a nonmonotone grid. Keeping existing auto exclusions and adding the bar-95 rule gives 227 extra inferred hand catches and 102 extra left-alone exclusions in W_D, so its first-step cost is 0.449339. Replacing the existing rule is a different experiment because some existing auto selections may fall below the new threshold.

For equal per-home values, evaluate `R * incremental_catches - incremental_over_applies` for every candidate and today's rule. On W_D, the 85-to-80 bundle costs 253/102=2.480392. Its two parts cost 99/41=2.414634 and 154/61=2.524590. The small separation is not evidence of a special optimum at 83. At the lower end, W_D's 65-to-55 bundle costs 452/72=6.277778, so the greedy rule would wrongly stop at 65 for some R values where 55 has higher total utility. Ties should be displayed as indifference, not precision about a uniquely chosen bar.

**Do not use the loss branch's $52 as a price for widening.** Even the historical all-age W_L estimate depends on an exposure denominator multiplied by the claims join. On the supplied totals, wind/hail paid A/E is 0.130091 and control paid A/E is 0.769585, giving a relative ratio of 0.169040. These are descriptive, conditional arithmetic. Illustratively, holding the reported roof ratio fixed while moving the control ratio from 0.4 to 1.2 yields relative gaps of 67.48% to 89.16%. That illustration is not a CI. Only a full policy-clustered bootstrap that also re-estimates comparator rates can supply uncertainty.

The source's latest-file dedupe is directionally right but needs two repairs. It filters missing dwelling IDs before choosing the latest claim, which can preserve an older key when the newest snapshot no longer has one. It also leaves ties within the same file unspecified. The new census tests both; these are blockers if present, not evidence that they occurred. A dwelling-only join can match more than one policy term. The executed W_E queries found zero ambiguous claim-home matches. The future guard remains: any nonzero count blocks quoting until a policy/term key is verified.

The source's exposure window includes `loss_date <= bind + exp_days` despite counting only the date difference as exposure. The proposal uses the explicit half-open interval `[start, end)` and reports its convention. Creation timestamp remains the NB cohort-entry filter, but exposure needs the agreed issue/effective start. Warehouse column comments resolve the exposure start as policy effective time and cancellation sequencing as quote effective time. The executed W_E query uses these fields, explicit America/Chicago dates, actual term end and the latest monthly claims horizon. A first-cancellation stop defines first-spell exposure; if reinstatements restore coverage, an active-interval sensitivity is needed.

**R needs a business unit.** Let B be the expected incremental value per decision-time quote of automating an exclusion an underwriter would otherwise apply: saved UW work, delay and any bind effect, with essentially no incremental coverage change if the counterfactual exclusion would happen anyway. Let C be the net expected incremental cost per decision-time quote of restricting a roof that would otherwise stay covered: bind contribution lost, expected removal/complaint/compliance cost, minus expected incremental insured-loss savings at that roof's age, score, coverage terms and persistence. Then R=B/C is usable only if C is positive and values are treated as constant across the compared band. If C is zero or negative, or values vary materially, directly compare expected utility by band. Historical dollars per excluded home-year must first be transported to marginal homes and converted using expected bind probability and covered duration; the historical loss join cannot do that conversion.

## What the decision needs

A catch means a home that a reviewer would exclude gets the same coverage earlier. Its benefit is a saved UW touch and any measured improvement in bind speed or conversion. It is not automatically an avoided-loss benefit, since the counterfactual already carried the exclusion. An over-apply changes coverage on a home the observed workflow left alone. It may avoid some paid loss, while imposing bind, removal, service, complaint and disclosure costs. The workflow label alone does not prove it was a mistake.

Use a common decision horizon and eligible quote population. Let B be the expected net dollars from an additional workflow catch and C the expected net cost of an additional over-apply, including any avoided loss. Then R = B/C has units of over-applies per catch only when C is positive. If C is zero or negative, the simple R stopping rule is not valid. Price every candidate, including status quo, by total expected net value. Do not use a greedy adjacent-step rule on a nonmonotone curve. The loss branch's historical all-age dollars per dwelling-year must first be re-estimated with corrected exposure, then adjusted for transport and uncertainty and multiplied by expected marginal bound exposure, before it can enter C. It cannot directly supply B or R.

For the May 1-Aug 15 2026 bound 101+ dwelling book, adding a score-95 rule while preserving existing auto coverage would add 227 inferred workflow catches and 102 left-alone exclusions. Its first added step costs 102/227 = 0.449 over-applies per catch, with 102/3.5 = 29.14 additional exclusions/month on that historical base. A replacement rule could also remove coverage from current auto homes below 95; their counterfactual requires a separate audit. The decision matrix's first priced move from 95 to 90 omits this distinction.

## Escape hatch measurement

The attestation asks replacement age while the score measures condition. Those are different facts. A sound slate, tile or metal roof older than 20 years may honestly answer no. A recent replacement can be defective. Evaluate those groups separately and give them a review path before assuming the question fixes false positives.

Run a shadow sample before removing coverage. Record eligible prompts, responses, accepts, explicit refusals, missing answers, later binds and elapsed time. Uptake is yes answers divided by all displayed prompts and separately all answers, by age/material/state and week. Compare score distributions for yes/no only after recording selection and missingness. Compare attestations to independently dated vendor/permit sources being evaluated in BUC-5223, preserving source coverage and confidence. Audit a random 5-10% of yes attestations with inspection or fresh imagery and a smaller no/missing control sample; report discrepancy and confirmed false-statement rates with intervals. A disagreement with a vendor is not automatically dishonesty.

Before launch set a practical uptake tripwire and a discrepancy-rate limit with UW. Do not invent measured thresholds from this repo. Track repeat yes-attestation frequency by agent in the private environment using a minimum-volume rule and case review, never publish agent rankings or names. Separate repeat legitimate business from abuse. Predefine when the pilot pauses, who reviews an image-age dispute, and which evidence permits a coverage decision.

The no-incremental-NOC claim depends on this unmeasured lane. Compare letter issuance, effective cancellations, reinstatements, non-roof cancellations, complaints and coverage removals on fixed 90/180-day follow-up. Also track acceptance and bind within a fixed conversion window for all eligible quotes, with agent/policy clustering as appropriate. A shadow study measures predictive behavior; it cannot estimate customer response to a coverage change. A controlled pilot is needed for that effect.

## Uncertainty and pilot endpoints

The first read's near-threshold p-values are 0.051 (45-day removals), about 0.07 (all-age score-matched bind), 0.061 (90+ roof-cancellation comparison), and 0.078 (pooled 83+ comparison). These do not establish a safe bound, equivalence or no effect. Dwellings from one policy are not independent trials. Fisher tests on unique policy counts do not gain a second correction for duplicated dwellings; dwelling-level tests need cluster resampling or policy aggregation first. Recompute the whole curve, chosen thresholds, step ratios and R windows in each policy-bootstrap draw, showing zero-catch steps as undefined/unbounded rather than a free step.

Predeclare the pilot's primary customer endpoint as bind within a fixed window among all eligible randomized quotes, and its primary operational endpoint as UW touches avoided per eligible quote. Predeclare noninferiority margins and sample size before seeing outcomes. Use attestation discrepancy, complaint/removal, NOC-letter and effective-cancellation measures as guardrails with separate maturity clocks. For losses bootstrap policy clusters and refit expected rates every draw; publish dollar A/E and netted-ratio intervals, capped-severity sensitivities and the control-peril sign test. A significant control-peril shift weakens the claimed roof-specific mechanism.
